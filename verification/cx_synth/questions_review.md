# Independent review of the Verus open-question answers
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)

Reviewed `out/vlm_questions/README.md` and all three linked reports on 2026-11-03. Sources were fetched directly, not accepted from the reports. Downloads, extracted text, repro source and logs are in `questions_evidence/` beside this file. “Confirmed” below means the cited source supports the claim; it does not mean I reproduced a published system's entire verification experiment.

## Findings requiring correction

1. **Wrong: “largest is Anvil, ~3.9K exec”.** The [Atmosphere SOSP 2025 paper](https://mars-research.github.io/doc/2025-sosp-atmo.pdf), abstract, says: “development and verification of 6K lines of executable code required an effort of less than 2.5 person-years”. It also confirms “a proof-to-code ratio of 3.32:1” and “less than 20 seconds on a modern laptop”. Q2 already mentions these numbers as unverified; they are now confirmed. The [VeruSAGE paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18436) does say “Anvil is by far the largest Verus-verified system”, but that does not establish that its executable code exceeds Atmosphere's. Specify the size measure and publication rather than declare a current maximum.

2. **Wrong/overstated: Q2's “whole projects verify in seconds to ~9 min ... ~1-3 min with parallelism”.** [Anvil Table 1](https://tianyin.github.io/pub/anvil.pdf) says “Total (all) 2326 3940 23975 1208 (401)”. These are 20.13 minutes serial and 6.68 minutes parallel for the three-controller total, excluding the separately described framework. Individual controller times are 520 (154), 341 (151), and 347 (96) seconds. The README's “~20 min” is correct for that aggregate. “Less than half an hour” is consistent with these observations, not refuted or inherently “too pessimistic”.

3. **Wrong as current evidence: Q1's “open expand-errors bugs”.** The cited issues [1214](https://github.com/verus-lang/verus/issues/1214), [1220](https://github.com/verus-lang/verus/issues/1220), and [1552](https://github.com/verus-lang/verus/issues/1552) all return `"state": "closed"`, `"state_reason": "completed"` from GitHub's API. Closure dates are 2024-07-08, 2024-12-21, and 2025-04-10. Their historical bug descriptions are genuine, but do not establish current defects. I reran #1552's `assert(false && false) by (nonlinear_arith)` with `--expand-errors` on Verus 0.2026.11.02.8bf68ee. It reports “error: assertion failed”; the erroneous “false ✔” expansion is absent. See `questions_evidence/expand.rs` and `expand.log`. Remove “has bugs” from the README unless accompanied by a current repro.

4. **Overstated: README's bare “~38% success on liveness”.** [TLA-Verus](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14923v1), Tables 8–9, gives 809 well-posed algorithmic liveness translations and 304 proven translations: 37.58%, as Q3 correctly calculates. This is corpus proof yield for that pipeline. Its separate model evaluation sums “58” solved outcomes out of “110” across eleven models on ten algorithmically translated liveness tasks. Label the denominator and experiment; do not present 38% as a general agent liveness success rate.

5. **Overstated: “TRUE for timeouts (no obligation named)” as a response to “Doesn't tell you anything about whether something went wrong in this particular function”.** Verus's [verifier source](https://github.com/verus-lang/verus/blob/main/source/rust_verify/src/verifier.rs) constructs `format!("{}: resource limit (rlimit) exceeded", context.desc)` and reports it at `&context.span`. A timeout does not identify a false obligation, but still identifies a checking context. Failure to prove is not evidence of a program defect.

6. **Overstated: Q3's “first public LLM liveness paper appears Oct 2026”.** The report expressly used only “a few queries”. Existence and date of this paper are confirmed; priority is not. Keep “one paper found”, and retain the admission that no causal explanation of absent real-system results was established.

## Q1: claim-by-claim checks

- **Confirmed: precondition, postcondition, assertion and arithmetic errors include source spans.** I constructed `questions_evidence/feedback.rs` and ran `tools/verus-x86-linux/verus out/cx_synth/questions_evidence/feedback.rs`. The log contains “error: precondition not satisfied”, “failed precondition”, “error: postcondition not satisfied”, “failed this postcondition”, “error: possible arithmetic underflow/overflow”, and “error: assertion failed”, each with the relevant file and expression. The installed binary emits a glibc weak-version warning but runs and produces verification results. This directly supports the README's main correction to the meeting claim.
- **Confirmed, scoped: approximately two errors per function by default.** The five-assertion `several` function emits two failed assertions and “not all errors may have been reported; rerun with a higher value for --multiple-errors”. The executable's help says “default: 2”. Do not interpret this as a guarantee of all failing obligations or every diagnostic category.
- **Confirmed: `--expand-errors`, profiling, timing and log flags exist.** Installed help says “When the proof fails, try to minimize the failing predicate”, “Collect and report prover performance data when resource limits are hit”, “Measure and report time taken”, “Log SMT queries”, and “Log complete SMT transcript”. Historical issue descriptions are confirmed; their present-tense interpretation is not (finding 3).
- **Confirmed, scoped: a model is discarded on one normal invalid-result branch; no dedicated counterexample-printing flag appears in the CLI.** Fetched `verifier.rs` contains `ValidityResult::Invalid(Some(_air_model), Some(error), assert_id_opt)`. This does not prove a useful model exists on every failure. The report's separate `evaluate_bool`/AIR implementation statements were not independently checked; **unverifiable in this review**. Its proposed connection to error expansion remains an inference.
- **Confirmed: timeout diagnostic and profile recommendation.** Source contains “resource limit (rlimit) exceeded” and “consider rerunning with `--profile` for more details”. **Overstated:** an absolute absence of obligation localization or feedback across every timeout mode; it depends on checking context.
- **Confirmed: AutoVerus uses error-specific repair and a generic fallback.** [AutoVerus](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.13082), debugging section, says it “collects all the verification errors”, “picks one error”, and prompts a suitable repair agent. It describes “the code snippet and detailed information about an error” and instructions for that error type; `repair_default` is explicitly named. Its ablation says the generic design takes “the whole Verus error report” and succeeds on “17 out of 37 tasks”. The report correctly limits those 37 to tasks solved through AutoVerus debugging. The source also explicitly states the claimed bound-error priority and subsequent invariant priority. Its algorithm line numbers differ from the report's rendering; this is not a behavioral discrepancy.
- **Unverifiable here:** ProofPlumber's quoted explanation (not fetched), exact guide quotations, the absence of profiler feedback in all LLM papers, and the explicitly unchecked AlphaVerus/SAFE/VeruSAGE/etc. formats. The direct binary repro establishes the ordinary diagnostic behavior without relying on those guide quotations. The general “modern agent loop ... nothing more by default” goes beyond one recommendation and one system: **overstated** if intended universally.
- **Inference only:** exposing helpful semantic counterexamples is difficult, and large conjunctive assertions may need finer diagnostic localization. Neither unused model variables nor old bugs independently establishes implementation difficulty today.

## Q2: claim-by-claim checks

- **Confirmed:** all Anvil controller counts, times, effort and framework numbers in Q2 match the [primary PDF](https://tianyin.github.io/pub/anvil.pdf), Table 1 and §7.1. Exact supporting passages include “87% of proof functions verify in under ten CPU seconds”, “the slowest of them takes 120 CPU seconds”, “Implementing and verifying each controller takes around 2.5 person-months”, “proof-to-code ratio ranges from 4.5 to 7.4”, and “5353 lines of reusable lemmas and 7817 lines of trusted code”. Incremental example: “403 lines in total and 211 in the proof”. The caption specifies parallel times use “11 threads on 6 cores”. The stated under-three-minute result is per controller, not all controllers together.
- **Confirmed:** [Atmosphere project page](https://mars-research.github.io/projects/atmo/) says “On average our code has proof-to-code ratio of 7.5:1”. Its newer paper confirms 6K executable lines, 3.32:1, under 20 seconds and under 2.5 person-years. Distinguish the older project description from the newer paper; these are not one experiment.
- **Confirmed:** [VeruSAGE](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2512.18436) calls its input eight systems, splits Anvil into two benchmark projects, and says “we say VeruSAGE-Bench contains 9 projects”. Its VeriSMo exclusion and incomplete Atmosphere availability are supported. The “largest” quotation is genuine but not a sound current executable-LoC ranking.
- **Confirmed with clarification:** VeruSAGE Table 3's average 496 is specification lines; 947 is total lines. They are not executable/proof columns. The source labels the remaining average columns 23 proof-lemma, 27 proof-target, 401 other. Q2 admits extraction uncertainty; do not reuse those values as code/proof size.
- **Confirmed, scoped:** modular proof-call abstraction. VeruSAGE says it retains the callee signature “including its pre- and post-conditions, as that is all Verus needs to finish the proof”, replacing the body with `unimplemented!()` **and** `verifier::external_body`. Q2 omits the latter part. This is ordinary proof-function modularity, not a claim that all specification-function bodies are unnecessary.
- **Unverifiable in this review:** every SOSP'24-specific number/quote (6.1K/31K totals; individual table rows; AWS hardware; 3–61x, 24–61x and 10x speedups; SMT-query comparisons; 1.6 ms/push; Ironclad/Hyperkernel ratios; Microsoft integration). The cited primary PDF returned HTTP 403, including a retry. A fetched 2023 Verus paper is a different work and was not substituted as evidence. These figures should remain attributed historical reports, not independently confirmed findings here.
- **Unverifiable:** “no evidence ... >10K” as any comprehensive current survey; the report did not investigate several known systems and 2026 papers. It can honestly describe its own search result. **Inference only:** proof-writing, rather than solver time, is the binding scale limit. Ratios and timings do not establish a general ceiling. The “4–13x” range also omits Atmosphere's now-confirmed 3.32 ratio.

## Q3: claim-by-claim checks

- **Confirmed:** [IronFleet primary PDF](https://sigops.org/s/conferences/sosp/2015/current/2015-Monterey/250-hawblitzel-online.pdf), Figure 12, has IronRSL liveness 7869 proof lines/115 minutes; protocol 1202/4; refinement 3379/26; IronKV liveness 2093/23. It says “real systems required approximately 3.7 person-years.” This is combined system development effort, not liveness-only effort. The liveness row is largest/slowest within IronRSL's protocol layer, not its entire implementation.
- **Confirmed as historical author claims:** IronFleet's “first system” qualification and “Verdi does not prove any liveness properties.” These describe the 2015 comparison, not current Verdi descendants. Fair-network assumptions are explicit: IronKV's result holds “if the network is fair”; IronRSL assumes eventual synchrony for a live quorum.
- **Confirmed:** IronFleet directly supports timeout difficulty: “Liveness, in contrast, requires reasoning about infinite series of system states. Such reasoning creates challenges for automated theorem provers (§2.4), often causing the prover to time out rather than return a successful verification or a useful error message.” This supports a historical obstacle, not a demonstrated cause of present agent failures.
- **Confirmed:** Anvil proof counts and two-person-month/two-person-week effort descriptions match the primary paper, including “we spent around two person-months on verifying ESR” and reuse for the other controllers. Exact weak-fairness and testing quotes from the blocked USENIX login page were not fetched: **unverifiable here**.
- **Confirmed:** TLA-Verus exists at the stated URL; its title is “TLA-Verus: Generating and Solving Temporal Proof Obligations with LLM Agents”. The paper supports the stated algorithmic corpus counts, evaluation counts, ten-attempt budget, reviewer feedback, no-counterexample description, and specification rather than implementation scope. It says “Connecting implementations to the specifications via refinement proofs is left for future work.” The difficulty comparison is explicitly “Liveness is where strong models break first”. These are paper-reported results, not independently rerun experiments.
- **Confirmed, scoped:** the TLA-Verus limitation about distinguishing false statements from difficult true ones and its claim about fairness assumptions appear in the fetched paper. Its statements about TLC/TLAPS are the authors' motivation, not an independently checked impossibility theorem. “No wall-clock timeouts” is only a claim about what this report found in the paper; lack of a documented timeout does not establish absence of time limits.
- **Unverifiable:** Ivy reduction details, Kondo scope, TLAPS coverage, IC3+LLM coverage, ExVerus and TraceFix details, precise lab affiliations and intended “entire lab”, private/internal work, and the RVBench date claim. These were already marked partly or wholly unchecked; retain those caveats. VeruSAGE's inclusion of an Anvil controller is independently confirmed.
- **Unverifiable causal inference:** public agent liveness work is scarce because proofs are expensive or feedback is weak. The sources show cost and feedback limitations; no reviewed evidence separates those explanations from benchmark design, research priorities, or publication/search coverage. The report's explicit “No source says ... because X” caveat is appropriate.

The review executed only newly written local diagnostic probes; it did not run cloud-directory code, contact maintainers, or change the original reports.
