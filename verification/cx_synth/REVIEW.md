# Independent review of verification targets and prototypes
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)

The reports contain useful candidate problems and reproducible toy results, but several claims need correction before reuse. The most consequential errors concern the JavaScript checker, live-worker memory, and the meaning of the proposed specifications. Original reports were not edited.

## Prioritized findings

1. **The symbolic executor does not establish JavaScript safety.** `vlm_runtime/SUMMARY.md` says “snapshot-specialized → all 3 proved”. Independent counterexamples make the checker print “PROVED: no exception” while Node throws `ReferenceError` or `TypeError`: it ignores undeclared-variable errors and models JavaScript numbers as unbounded integers. The supplied good snapshot is safe under its assumptions, but the checker cannot support the general proof claim. Also, “bad snapshot → 2 real violations” mislabels array indexing as an exception: the actual script at `qty=4` prints “no exception; total=NaN”. The cookie read is real. **Classification: overstated proof claim; wrong exception claim.** [Code, counterexamples and correction](runtime_review.md); [raw output](dynstate_counterexamples.log).

2. **The worker-memory result measures exited workers.** `vlm_runtime/SUMMARY.md` says “worker isolate 0.78 MB”. At the original benchmark's measurement point, my instrumented rerun records `"exited_workers":50` for 50 workers. It therefore does not measure 50 live isolates. Context/process reruns give 441/9,225 KB each, roughly 21× for this toy Node workload. They do not establish that Chrome's memory is mostly DOM/layout/GPU or that browser savings would be small. **Classification: wrong live-isolate interpretation; unsupported browser extrapolation.** [Memory evidence](tabiso_memory.log), [review](runtime_review.md).

3. **Normative algorithms are being called executable specifications.** `vlm_targets/TARGETS.md` says “WHATWG/ECMA are executable specs” while proposing “a hand-transcribed spec”. The standards are algorithmic prose/pseudocode; the report identifies no executable reference semantics. Name the chosen implementation/transcription and its trust assumptions. Likewise, target 9 combines microtask-order conformance with application race freedom; compliant task scheduling does not establish order-independent results. **Classification: wrong specification description; overstated combined property.** [All 13 targets assessed](targets_review.md), including exact standards links and an independent task-order counterexample.

4. **The Verus scale ranking and current-bug evidence need updating.** `vlm_questions/README.md` says “largest is Anvil, ~3.9K exec”. The [Atmosphere paper](https://mars-research.github.io/doc/2025-sosp-atmo.pdf) reports “6K lines of executable code”; the ranking is wrong for executable size. The three cited `--expand-errors` bugs are closed; the #1552 example no longer exhibits its reported bad expansion on installed Verus. Ordinary failed assertions, requirements, postconditions and arithmetic obligations do produce source-located diagnostics in fresh runs. **Classification: wrong ranking/outdated bug evidence; confirmed ordinary diagnostics.** [Source audit and Verus logs](questions_review.md).

5. **Time and liveness summaries need their denominators.** Anvil's table gives “1208 (401)” seconds for all three controllers: about 20.1 minutes serial / 6.7 minutes parallel. The README's approximate 20 minutes is supported; the underlying report's nine-minute whole-project ceiling is not. TLA-Verus's approximately 38% is 304 proved / 809 well-posed algorithmic liveness translations, not a general agent success rate. Its paper says “Connecting implementations to the specifications via refinement proofs is left for future work.” **Classification: wrong underlying time ceiling; overstated liveness shorthand.** [Anvil paper](https://tianyin.github.io/pub/anvil.pdf), [TLA-Verus paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.14923v1), [detailed audit](questions_review.md).

6. **Novelty is not established by the reviewed sources.** The target list's “This would be the first” exceeds its cited “I didn't find any source”. The prior-work report documents relevant families, but neither an exhaustive search nor implementation of the proposed proof-and-guard combination. Its combined “checked rule by rule” description also understates current VeriISLE: the [project README](https://github.com/bytecodealliance/wasmtime/blob/main/cranelift/isle/veri/README.md) says “It analyzes chains of ISLE rules”. Dynamic Determinacy reports facts that “hold for any execution”, rather than a conditional proof valid only under a captured snapshot. **Classification: overstated novelty and categorization.** [Full prior-work audit](prior_review.md).

7. **One historical failure is quoted inaccurately.** `TARGETS.md` quotes “after write-barrier elision was already decided”, but those are the evidence file's agent-authored paraphrase, not Project Zero's words. The actual [CVE-2021-4102 analysis](https://googleprojectzero.github.io/0days-in-the-wild/0day-RCAs/2021/CVE-2021-4102.html) says: “A check before EarlyOptimizationPhase in SimplifiedLoweringPhase makes the correct assumption that write barriers can be elided when storing anything to a field in an object with the TaggedSigned representation.” The historical mechanism is supported; remove the false quotation. **Classification: wrong attribution, confirmed underlying mechanism.** [Target review](targets_review.md).

8. **Lightpanda’s comparison counts and termination label are wrong.** The summary says “8 DOM/JS-state match; 6 differ”, while both its saved comparator and the independent copied rerun print “7/14 pages differ”. That combined comparator includes network differences; the state comparator reports only three differences but deliberately skips timer-page state. “All terminate” means navigation/probe returned after a 400 ms wait, even for an explicitly recurring timer. Separately, a copied enforcement rerun confirms the narrow WebSocket request-filter gap: “B-hits 1 beacon_ws:1” under the blocking flag. **Classification: wrong counts; overstated termination; confirmed forbidden-host request for this build/pattern.** [Detailed review and evidence](lightpanda_review.md), [fresh comparison](lightpanda_copy/diff_rerun.log).

## Coverage and evidence

These companion reviews form the claim ledger for this review; each classifies claims as confirmed, wrong, overstated or unverifiable and distinguishes proposals from achieved results:

- [Targets](targets_review.md): all 13 candidates, takeaways, meeting references, proposed contracts and closing claims; fresh historical-source retrievals and two independent Node checks.
- [Prior work](prior_review.md): all six sections, publication/scope claims, differentiators and gap statement; independently fetched papers, repository sources and metadata.
- [Verus questions](questions_review.md): every README bullet and underlying claims; fresh Verus diagnostic probes, current issue states and primary-paper checks.
- [Runtime and tab isolation](runtime_review.md): copied-code reruns, semantic counterexamples, worker lifetime instrumentation, and fresh Rust compilation of the heap model.
- [Lightpanda](lightpanda_review.md): copied localhost repros, state comparisons, WebSocket filtering, source-size/dependency checks and claimed limitations.

Confirmed local results include the executor's 9/0/2 alarm counts (not their semantic soundness), 61.1 ms for the good toy snapshot, and the Rust model's “unchecked: 19975/20000 runs violate invariant” / “checked:   0/20000 runs violate invariant”. The Rust result is randomized testing of the stated ownership invariant, not a proof of engine isolation.

“Confirmed” for publications means the independently obtained source supports the statement; published experiments were not reproduced wholesale. Historical CVE descriptions are not current-engine exploit reproductions. “Unverifiable” identifies an evidence/access/scope limit, not proof that a claim is false. Recommendations such as trying Verus, adding guards, or comparing WPT subsets remain recommendations.
