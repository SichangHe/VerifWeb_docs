# Review of the advisor write-up
(authored by agents unless marked 🧑)

The draft answers the meeting's four asks, but needs factual corrections before advisor use. The largest problems are the ten-rule typer claim, the assertion that only directly verified Rust can connect models to implementations, the email destination claim, and the blanket Verus limitation. Several corrections already present in the cited reviews were lost in synthesis.

Reviewed [WRITEUP.md](../vlm4_writeup/WRITEUP.md), all its cited report groups, and the relevant detailed reviews. This is a claim-to-source audit, not another execution of the experiments. References below identify the reviewed draft's line numbers; quotes are from that version. No write-up or implementation was edited. Nothing under the email directory was written.

## Required corrections, in priority order

1. **High — the typer result omits a timeout and changes domains.** Draft line 19: “z3 finds the 10 integer range rules in V8's `operation-typer.cc` sound”. The cited [job2 review, B4](../vlm3_review/job2/REPORT.md) says: “In the main 64-bit-bitvector encoding Multiply was `unknown` (timeout). Only an unbounded-integer reformulation was `unsat`.” It also says: “Add/Sub/Mod inputs restricted to int32 ranges, `y==0` excluded for Mod”. The draft's exact-integer caveat does not disclose these restrictions or the changed multiplication encoding. Suggested replacement: “Z3 discharged nine rules in the restricted bit-vector model; multiplication timed out there but passed an unbounded-integer reformulation. Four deliberately broken variants produced counterexamples.” Keep the existing floating-point exclusions, and state the int32/nonzero-divisor restrictions. CVE-2021-21220 motivates a compiler/range mismatch, not a demonstrated defect in these transfer rules: the [target review, finding 7](../cx_synth/targets_review.md) explicitly says, “A correct abstract transfer function can disagree with incorrectly compiled concrete execution.” Replace “where that CVE lives” with “the generated-code behavior implicated in that CVE”.

2. **High — the model-to-code answer is false and internally contradictory.** Draft line 41: “does a proof about a model say anything about the C++? Only if the code is in Rust and verified directly”. The [target review's closing audit](../cx_synth/targets_review.md) already warns: “Verus proofs of Rust reimplementations still need correspondence to production Rust, just as C++ models do.” The cited [VeRA account](../cloud/p4_prior_work_check.md), §1, says: “Browser developers write range analysis routines in a subset of C++, and verification developers write infrastructure to verify custom analysis properties.” Language choice does not establish or preclude correspondence. Suggested replacement: “What connects the model to the implementation? We need a justified correspondence; our handwritten C++ and Rust models do not establish it. Verus may let us verify suitable Rust code more directly.”

3. **High — different domains were promoted into different companies, and non-detection into absence.** Draft line 33: “crawled 532 real sites”; “0 of 68 pages leaked the password”; “11 of 139 pages sent the email to another company”. The [crawl report](../vlm2_scripts/t12_offorigin/REPORT.md) says “532 URLs”, and defines its allowlist as “same registrable domain (tldts) as the final page URL”. It explicitly identifies “login.taobao.com -> mmstat.com” and says “mmstat is Alibaba's own analytics, so ‘third party’ is a same-company guess [BELIEF]”. It also excludes “salted/custom hashes, encryption, compressed/binary bodies” and other channels. Suggested replacement: “Across 532 URLs, we detected email-canary transmission to another registrable domain on 11 of 139 pages with email fields, before submission: eight hashed, three readable or reversibly encoded. We detected no password-canary transmission on 68 pages with password fields. Different domains need not mean different companies; the detector misses some encodings and channels.” For SRI, replace “Only 3.1% of cross-site script tags had SRI hashes” with “215 of 6,894 observed cross-site script tags had an `integrity` attribute (3.1%).” The report counts an attribute, not validated matching hashes, and excludes dynamic tags absent from the final DOM.

4. **High — the tool limitation contradicts the controlling review.** Draft line 51: “It doesn't handle floats or interior mutability (`Cell`, atomics)”. The [Servo review, remaining summary claims](../cx_servo/REVIEW.md) says: “‘interior mutability ... is unsupported’ conflicts with the component report's own mention of vstd atomic permissions and PCell alternatives.” Suggested replacement: “These attempts omitted floating-point code and did not verify the existing interior-mutability code; raw-pointer operations required rewrites and trusted contracts.” This reports the experiments without claiming Verus universally lacks those capabilities.

5. **Medium — the cost claim is unmeasured and too broad.** Draft line 51: “Verus worked on small arithmetic and structural Rust in real crates in about half an hour of agent time each.” The [job2 review, A9](../vlm3_review/job2/REPORT.md) labels the original 25–35 minute claim “UNVERIFIABLE (no logs of agent time)”. [The later Servo summary](../vlm3_servo/SUMMARY.md) says: “each agent took about 1.5–2.5 h of wall-clock time over 2–3 rounds”. These are transcriptions and rewrites, not a uniform measurement of directly verifying crate code. Suggested replacement: “Verus worked on small arithmetic and structural models derived from real crates.” Omit time, or restrict it to the self-reported first-round estimates.

6. **Medium — the sandbox property excludes legitimate external accesses.** Draft line 24: “Sandboxed code only touches its own memory.” The [target review, finding 5](../cx_synth/targets_review.md) says: “V8 explicitly seeks to ‘prevent memory corruption outside of the sandbox,’ not to forbid every legitimate mediated access outside it.” The [EPT report](../vlm2_iso/sandbox_verus/REPORT.md) says its result is “a valid payload of an entry whose tag is exactly x” and that “‘right object’ is not stated”. Suggested replacement: “Sandboxed code cannot cause unauthorized memory accesses.” Describe the EPT experiment as a tag-checking model under trusted-write and tag-encoding assumptions; it does not establish the whole confinement property. The Wasm bounds-check result and 35-bit historical quote are supported.

7. **Medium — the draft erases the distinction between bug evidence and full-engine execution.** Draft line 11: “the checks found eight bugs in real code”; line 65: “All reproduced by an independent rerun. Nothing has been filed upstream.” There are eight listed defects, but not eight newly discovered or end-to-end engine reproductions. The [bug review](../cx_bugs/REVIEW.md) says “Full Servo JavaScript execution was not performed”; its [Servo detail](../cx_bugs/servo_review.md) says the MP4 harness “includes the pinned real source and stubs only `LoadContext`”. The same review confirms existing upstream issues for MP4 and cssparser. Suggested opening: “The experiments and source inspection identified eight defects, including previously reported ones; independent reviews reproduced each at the evidence level listed below.” Suggested section introduction: “Independent reruns reproduced the crate, harness, or copied-code failures below. We have not filed any new upstream reports.” In the MP4 bullet add “in a harness using the original source; Servo was not built”. This also removes the apparent contradiction between “Nothing has been filed upstream” and “Already reported”.

8. **Medium — practical unreachability is not established.** Draft line 74: “Real, not practically reachable.” The [Servo bug review, §2](../cx_bugs/servo_review.md) says: “Neither browser limits nor real web reachability were measured; preserving the explicit belief/untested qualification is necessary.” Suggested replacement: “Reproduced with a 4 GiB input; practical web reachability was not tested.” The [Servo review](../cx_servo/REVIEW.md) also specifies: “The first overflow is at the 2^32-th newline, starting from zero.” Use “at 2^32 newlines” rather than “after”.

9. **Medium — VeRA's scope is broader in the draft than in the paper.** Draft line 61: “VeRA (PLDI 2020) verified Firefox's range analysis”. The [prior-work review, §1](../cx_synth/prior_review.md) quotes: “We also do not verify the range analysis algorithm itself nor Firefox’s use of ranges in code generation or other optimization passes (e.g., DCE or BCE).” It also quotes “137 conditions out of a possible 159”. Suggested replacement: “VeRA (PLDI 2020) verified selected Firefox range-analysis routines; it did not verify the full analysis or its consumers.” The future-work statement is supported. Change “the closest is TurboTV” to “a related system is TurboTV”: the same review says the sources do not establish “uniquely closest” rankings. TurboTV's year and loop-free compilation-validation scope are supported.

10. **Medium — the cross-phase example implies a closer CVE match than was demonstrated.** Draft line 20 places “CVE-2021-4102” immediately before “a Verus proof on a toy IR”. The [job2 review, B8](../vlm3_review/job2/REPORT.md) explains: “The mechanism (value representation / type change vs young-allocation recency), pass (SimplifiedOperatorReducer vs allocation folding) and invariant differ.” The draft does not explicitly claim mechanism equivalence, but its presentation invites that reading. Suggested addition: “The toy example uses allocation folding across a call, a different mechanism in the same cross-phase bug family.” The historical barrier-elision account, passing proof, failing mutant and four-instruction witness are otherwise supported.

11. **Medium — “there is none” overstates the absence of engine specifications.** Draft line 40: “For engine internals (items 1-4) there is none; it's implicit in the C++.” The [target review](../cx_synth/targets_review.md) says: “The claim that all engine invariants are ‘undocumented’ is overstated, especially when the cited RCAs document several.” Suggested replacement: “For these engine models, we wrote contracts from implementation behavior and documented invariants; we have not established their correspondence to the production code. Web standards provide prose algorithms.”

12. **Medium — page-state dependence is the meeting idea; the guard is a proposal added afterward.** Draft line 11: “the one raised in the meeting: verify a script for the page state you actually have, and add a runtime guard”. Speaker 5's actual [meeting transcript](/ssd1/sichangheagent/email/1103madhyastha_govindan_ghavidel.vtt:503) says: “And the runtime verification has the dynamic state context, and that's the difference. The question is, for the given state, what is the behavior of this? As opposed to, for any given state, what is the behavior?” That passage does not specify a snapshot or invalidating guard. Suggested replacement: “Building on the meeting's given-state question, I propose a proof conditioned on a page-state snapshot, with a guard that invalidates its use when assumptions change.” Define what “safe” means, choosing one of the proposed properties; also state what execution does after invalidation. A guard that merely withdraws a proof does not by itself prevent unsafe execution. This is an unresolved design question, not evidence the proposal cannot work.

## Smaller scope and writing corrections

- **Side effects, line 18.** “any IR node that can run user code invalidates the type facts inferred before it” is a conservative candidate policy, not the definition of soundness. The [target review](../cx_synth/targets_review.md) says invalidating everything is “stronger than necessary when writes cannot affect the relevant object”. Prefer “invalidate any inferred heap/type facts that the user code may change”. “All op sequences” means the nine-opcode abstract alphabet: [job2 B2](../vlm3_review/job2/REPORT.md) says “9 abstract opcodes whose write-effects were hand-assigned” and “shows consistency with the CVE, not an independent discovery”. Say “all sequences of up to four modeled operations”; call 396/0 model results, and replace “current main” with “the checked main revision”.
- **Boa counts, lines 29 and 68.** Name the tested executor. The [loop review's rerun](../cx_loop/REVIEW.md) reports “boa-simple total 9430 fail(es2019) 4171”, but “boa-cli total 9430 fail(es2019) 9”. Use “Boa's default SimpleJobExecutor disagreed on 4,171 of 9,430 programs”. This does not affect the supported cancellation regression or patch result. The draft's model-to-code caveat is correct.
- **Blob trigger, line 71.** “about 1,024 chained slices” omits the large offsets essential to the trigger. The [Servo bug review](../cx_bugs/servo_review.md) says “the 1025th large addition after the initial `slice(1)`”. Prefer “about 1,024 chained `slice(1e19)` calls after `slice(1)` overflow the copied arithmetic”. Keep the existing no-Servo-build caveat.
- **Bitset, line 70.** Specify `ScalarBitSet::<u64>` and scope release behavior to tested builds. The [bitset review](../cx_bugs/bitset_review.md) says “Our release executions supply that separate evidence on this compiler/host.” The debug panic, observed full-set result, and predecessor PR #6020 are supported.
- **“Test262/WPT-style”, line 51.** The [loop review](../cx_loop/REVIEW.md) explicitly says “Full tests/test262 remain unverified”; the draft supplies custom generated-program evidence. Prefer “differential testing of generated programs across engines”. Naming standard suites adds ambiguity without explaining the method.
- **Historical motivation, lines 11 and 33.** “each tied to a bug” does not mean each experiment models that bug. The tab example is a meeting scenario; its historical isolation motivation is absent from this draft. Polyfill is supported as malicious third-party script distribution, not specifically input exfiltration: the [target review](../cx_synth/targets_review.md) says “These do not establish payment-field exfiltration in every case.” The [original British Airways source note](../vlm_targets/src_programs.md) says “attacker edited a first-party static script served on the payment flow”. Use “motivated by reported failures” in the opening; distinguish compromised served scripts from third-party-hosted scripts in item 10.
- **Clarity and duplication.** “safe”, “drops the proof”, “fixpoint”, “Typer”, “SRI”, and “canary” need a concrete property or a short definition where first used. For this systems audience, define “canary” as a unique test value and SRI as a script-integrity attribute; explain that invalidation removes permission to rely on a proof. Items 4, 7 and 8 repeat the bug section; keep the target/property and refer to the detailed results once. The “Correctness issues by layer” list repeats the ten-target grouping without adding a question. These are editorial recommendations, not factual errors.
- **Sources are not usable advisor citations.** Lines 78–86 give internal paths in backticks, grouped by many claims. Link the corresponding report sections, and use primary issue/paper links for historical bugs and prior work. Do not require advisors to know the workspace directory layout. The assertion “Where a review corrected a report, I used the review” should be retained only after the corrections above are made.

## Remaining claim coverage and limits

- **Meeting quotes, lines 7–9 and 25:** checked against the transcript, read-only. Speaker 5 says “Write down 10 different things that you might like to verify, either about individual JavaScripts or the engine itself.” Speaker 1 says “you need to have some differentiator”; Speaker 4 says “if we run two tabs in a single process”. The questions/correctness/tools quote spans Speaker 5's adjacent statements. These quotations are supported; no speaker-name mapping was inferred.
- **GC motivation and result, lines 21 and 67:** [cx_gc](../cx_gc/REVIEW.md) confirms “live objects may remain unmarked and be collected, leading to use-after-free” and “all 9 report” Miri use-after-free across the three versions and three modes. “Safe Rust only” is supported for the reproducer. No-report-found remains a limited search result, not novelty. “main” means the tested revision. The abstract GC goal does not imply a complete proof of Boa.
- **Isolation and rooting, lines 24–25 and 30:** the cited [sandbox](../vlm2_iso/sandbox_verus/REPORT.md), [tab](../vlm2_iso/tabiso_verus/REPORT.md), and [rooting](../vlm2_iso/servo_rooting/REPORT.md) reports support their reported pass/fail results and stated model boundaries. The tab report says “any heap size and any op sequence” under its invariant-preserving operations; it also says “fields are append-only”. The rooting report says “Kani separates the #24621 pattern ... from a compliant Drop”. These are source-supported model claims, not independently rerun in this audit. The Wasmtime and Servo historical accounts are also corroborated by the target review.
- **Parsers and alternatives, lines 28 and 35:** [the target review](../cx_synth/targets_review.md) corroborates Tsai's quotation, lodash CVE-2019-10744, DOMPurify mXSS, and the Cloudflare 2019 regex outage. Standards-as-prose is accurately qualified. The two cssparser and two Servo findings are supported at the evidence levels above. “Not tried” is an author-history claim, not independently authenticated.
- **Agent contribution, line 43:** the [Servo review](../cx_servo/REVIEW.md) demonstrates the false trusted assumption: “I ... proved `assert(false)`.” The cited [fix report](../vlm4_sbvfix/REPORT.md) subsequently says “the counterexample no longer verifies; the full proof re-verifies unchanged in scope”. The draft's past-tense claim is accurate. “Agents did all the model building” is reported provenance, not something these artifacts independently authenticate.
- **Differentiator and prior work, lines 55–61:** CoreJIT/POPL 2021, Staged Information Flow/PLDI 2009 and Dynamic Determinacy/PLDI 2013 are corroborated by [the prior review](../cx_synth/prior_review.md). “Analysis seeded from one run” is defensible shorthand; it must not become a claim that Dynamic Determinacy proves only the captured state. Its abstract says the inferred facts “hold for any execution”. Negative searches are appropriately labeled “I found no”; the search's completeness and exact history remain unverifiable. The [runtime review](../cx_synth/runtime_review.md) supports the unsound-executor admission with actual ReferenceError/TypeError counterexamples and says “no real-script measurements”. It also says “no mutation guard”; adding that explicit missing part would make the proposal's status clearer.
- **Remaining bug bullets, lines 69, 72–73:** [the Lightpanda review](../cx_bugs/lightpanda_review.md) confirms the HTTP/WS split on its pinned nightly, with “fetch0”:“blocked”, “xhr0”:“blocked” and “ws”:“hello-from-18003”. [The Servo review](../cx_bugs/servo_review.md) confirms the MP4 panic and cssparser delimiter mismatch and the existing issue states at its check date. These do not establish present-day behavior for every build. Upstream non-filing is supported as the reports' declared conduct, not independently provable from code.

Recommendation: correct the factual and scope errors, retain the ten-target structure, and present the snapshot-plus-guard idea as a proposed next experiment with a named property and explicit failure behavior. No additional benchmark reruns are needed to make these source-supported corrections.
